    Just a starting point. These are general suggestions, not medical or safety advice. Everyone runs a little warmer or colder, so adjust to how you actually feel and check the conditions around you before heading out.

    How the tool decides

    It starts from the temperature you enter. Wind makes cool days feel colder, so on days under 70°F it knocks off about 5°F for a breeze and 10°F for strong wind before picking a row. On warm days wind barely changes the outfit. If you entered a "feels like" temperature, leave wind on Calm so it isn't counted twice.

    80°F and upT-shirt, shorts or a light dress, sneakers or sandals. Sun hat and a water bottle. At 90°F and up, plan on shade and water breaks.
    75–79°FShort sleeves and shorts. No jacket.
    65–74°FT-shirt with a light hoodie or long-sleeve layer you can take off by midday.
    55–64°FLong sleeves, long pants, and a light jacket or fleece.
    45–54°FLong sleeves plus a sweater or fleece, under a medium-weight jacket. Closed-toe shoes.
    32–44°FWarm layer under a winter coat. Add a hat and gloves once it's below 40°F.
    20–31°FBase layer, warm mid layer, heavy winter coat, hat, gloves or mittens, scarf or neck gaiter, warm socks and boots.
    Below 20°FAll of the above with a thermal base layer and snow pants. Cover ears, hands and face, and keep outdoor time short.

    Rain: "Might rain" means pack an umbrella or a packable rain jacket. "Raining" means a hooded rain jacket (or a waterproof coat on cold days), rain boots or waterproof shoes, and a spare pair of socks in your bag. At or below freezing, wet weather can turn into sleet or snow, so swap rain boots for insulated snow boots and pack waterproof mittens.

    Wind: a zip-up outer layer that blocks the wind beats a thicker sweater. When it's really windy and wet, a hood is easier than an umbrella that keeps flipping inside out.

    Commuting: on cold days, wear layers you can open or take off once you are on a warm bus or train or in a heated office, rather than one heavy sweater under the coat.
